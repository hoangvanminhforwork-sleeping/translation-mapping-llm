Immigration continued to play a crucial role throughout the ensuing centuries, each wave of newcomers bringing their own culinary traditions. The 19th and early 20th centuries witnessed a massive influx of immigrants from Italy, Germany, Ireland, Eastern Europe, and China. These groups introduced their native foods, which over time, assimilated into the broader American diet. Italian immigrants, for instance, popularized pasta and pizza, which have become central components of American fare. Similarly, the Chinese brought dishes that have been adapted into Americanized forms, like chop suey and General Tso’s chicken.

Additionally, African influences on American cuisine cannot be overlooked. Enslaved Africans brought with them knowledge of their native cooking techniques and ingredients, which significantly impacted Southern cuisine. Dishes such as gumbo, jambalaya, and various barbeque techniques owe much to African culinary heritage. Moreover, the practice of using spices and creating rich, flavorful dishes is a direct legacy of African influence.
