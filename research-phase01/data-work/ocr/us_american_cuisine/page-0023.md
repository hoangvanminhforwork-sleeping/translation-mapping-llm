Similarly, Chinese immigrants who arrived during the Gold Rush and later to work on the Transcontinental Railroad introduced Chinese cuisine, which soon captivated American taste buds. Early establishments known as "chop suey houses" adapted traditional dishes to suit local ingredients and American preferences. This led to the creation of hybrid dishes that bore only a passing resemblance to their Chinese origins but became wildly popular. Today, American-Chinese dishes like General Tso's chicken and fortune cookies are favorites at Chinese restaurants nationwide, showcasing how immigrant food can transform and thrive in a new environment.
