The New England states, with their harsh winters, developed a cuisine based on preserved meats, root vegetables, and seafood, exemplified by dishes like clam chowder and lobster rolls. In contrast, the Southern states, blessed with a longer growing season, benefitted from a variety of fresh produce, resulting in a cuisine heavy on vegetables, chicken, pork, and rice dishes.

Regional diversity also speaks to the integration of native and immigrant foodways. In the Southwest, Native American, Spanish, and Mexican culinary traditions converged, giving rise to Tex-Mex cuisine, which features ingredients like beans, chili peppers, tortillas, and cheese. The Midwest, often regarded as the American heartland, developed a cuisine centered on agricultural products like corn, wheat, beef, and dairy. Here, homestyle dishes, such as casseroles and pot roasts, typify the region's straightforward, hearty approach to food.
