## Chapter 2 : The Rise of Industrialized Food – Convenience and Standardization

The rise of industrialized food in America marks a significant chapter in the evolution of the nation's dietary habits. Industrialization brought sweeping changes to the production, distribution, and consumption of food, fundamentally altering the landscape of American cuisine. The advent of industrialization saw traditional, labor-intensive methods of food preparation and preservation making way for mass production and mechanization. Factories started churning out vast quantities of food products, making them more accessible to an ever-growing urban population.

One of the most notable impacts of industrialization on American food was the growth of processed foods and the emergence of national brands. The late 19th and early 20th centuries witnessed the advent of products like canned goods, breakfast cereals, and convenience foods, which promised to save time and effort in the kitchen.
