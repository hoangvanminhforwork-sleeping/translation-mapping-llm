## About the book

In "The Food of a Younger Land," Mark Kurlansky takes readers on a captivating culinary journey through America’s regional foodways, immersing us in a mosaic of flavors, traditions, and stories that define the nation’s cultural landscape before the advent of fast food and corporate dining. Drawing from a treasure trove of long-lost writings from the WPA (Works Progress Administration), Kurlansky resurrects the unique tastes and culinary practices that gave life to local communities, inviting us to savor the past and appreciate the richness of American cuisine in its most authentic form. As we explore the diverse palates of a bygone era, we are reminded of how food can tell the story of a people, their struggles, triumphs, and an ever-evolving identity—an invitation to rediscover what truly sustains us.

About the author

Mark Kurlansky is an esteemed American author and journalist, celebrated for his engaging narrative style and in-depth exploration of culinary history and culture. Born on December 7, 1948, in Hartford, Connecticut, Kurlansky’s diverse writing encompasses various subjects, including food, economics, and global history, with a penchant for uncovering the connections between everyday life and broader historical themes. He is perhaps best known for bestselling works such as "Salt: A World History" and "Cod: A Biography of the Fish That Changed the World," where he adeptly blends personal anecdotes with historical analysis. In "The Food of a Younger Land," Kurlansky further showcases his passion for food as a lens to view American life, mining rare historical sources to illustrate the rich tapestry of regional cuisines and the profound impact of food on culture and identity.
